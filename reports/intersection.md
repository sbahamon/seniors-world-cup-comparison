## Which filter is doing the work

At the 30% coverage cut. 'Rescued by RSSSF' is the count that
decides whether RSSSF is worth doing: squads that fail coverage but pass
precision, i.e. the ones a supplementary source would actually add to the
usable set. Squads failing precision are not rescued by any source --
their federation did not play enough in-window youth editions to measure.

| K | pass both | fail cov only (RSSSF rescues) | fail precision only | fail both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| >=4 | 54 | 25 | 40 | 33 |
| >=5 | 40 | 18 | 54 | 40 |
| >=6 | 28 | 12 | 66 | 46 |

As a share of the badly-covered squads RSSSF would target:

- K>=4: 25 of 58 badly-covered squads pass precision (43%); the other 33 are unmeasurable regardless of coverage.
- K>=5: 18 of 58 badly-covered squads pass precision (31%); the other 40 are unmeasurable regardless of coverage.
- K>=6: 12 of 58 badly-covered squads pass precision (21%); the other 46 are unmeasurable regardless of coverage.

At K>=5 the 5 federations RSSSF would add: Canada, China PR, Costa Rica, New Zealand, Paraguay.

### What the surviving set is made of

The two filters are not independent of the thing being measured.
Article coverage tracks national wealth and European club presence;
qualification frequency tracks youth-programme investment. Both select
the same federations, so the intersection is not a random subsample of
world football -- it is close to a list of well-resourced federations.
Any finding computed on it is a finding about that set, and cannot
speak to whether youth pipelines feed senior success in general.

- men: 35 squads, 14 distinct federations, across 4 senior editions
- women: 5 squads, 4 distinct federations, across 2 senior editions
