## 2. Are the recovered players actually alumni?

The coverage diagnostic assumed redlinked players are lost *alumni* — that
closing the gap would raise measured overlap. That assumption has never been
tested, and it is testable directly: take each recovered QID and ask whether
it turns up in a senior squad for the same federation inside the window.
Senior-side coverage is 99.7% (11 redlinks in 4,327 players), so a recovered
player who converted would be found.

**2 of 184 recovered players (1.1%) appear in a senior squad inside their window. 182 (98.9%) do not.**

For comparison, the same measurement over the youth players who were *already* linked on en.wiki: **1057/8666 = 12.2%**.

If recovered players converted at the rate of already-covered ones we would expect about **22** of the 184, not 2. Under a binomial with p=0.122, P(X<=2) = 1.42e-08.

### What this bounds for the 4,304 redlinks still unresolved

At the observed rate, the remaining 4304 youth redlinks would contribute
about **47** further alumni across all 184 senior squads — roughly
0.25 per squad, against the 5.4 per squad measured now.
At the *already-covered* rate of 12.2% it would instead be about
525, which is the number the coverage worry has implicitly assumed.

Which of those two is the right projection depends on whether the recovered
players resemble the residue. They almost certainly overstate it: a player
notable enough for an article on their home wiki is more likely to have
converted than one with no article on any wiki. So **1.1% reads as an upper
bound on the residue's conversion rate, not a central estimate** — which makes
the projection above an upper bound too.

The mechanism is worth naming, because it means the redlink gap is not a
neutral veil over the outcome. English Wikipedia notability for a youth
footballer is driven substantially by reaching a senior national team, so
converting is itself a large part of *why* a player stops being a redlink.
The redlinked pool is therefore depleted of alumni by construction. That is a
reason the gap costs less than feared — and simultaneously a reason it can
never be treated as missing-at-random.
